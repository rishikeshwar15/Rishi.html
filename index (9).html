Srinivasa Ramanujan

Life History

Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920) was an extraordinary Indian mathematician born in Erode, Tamil Nadu. From a very young age, he displayed exceptional talent in mathematics, independently discovering complex formulas and theorems despite having very little formal education. His work gained international recognition when he contacted British mathematician G.H. Hardy, who invited him to Cambridge University. During his short life, Ramanujan made remarkable contributions to number theory, infinite series, continued fractions, and modular forms. His notebooks, containing thousands of original results, continue to inspire mathematicians around the world.

Awards

  1. Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS), 1918
  2. Ramanujan Prize for young mathematicians
  3. Commemorative coins and stamps
  4. Institutions named after him

Key Achievements

  • Displayed extraordinary mathematical talent from childhood
  • Compiled nearly 3,900 formulas and theorems
